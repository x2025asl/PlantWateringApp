# Business case

Your prototype shows *what* the product looks like. This document explains *why* it could become a business. Answer in your own words and refer to course concepts by name. See the term project brief on Moodle for length and grading.

## 1. The idea

- **Customer:** People who keep several houseplants but do not have a reliable routine for remembering each plant's different watering schedule.
- **Problem:** A generic weekly reminder is too broad: some plants are already overdue while others need little water. Owners can forget care or overwater plants, and common solutions include memory, paper notes, or separate phone reminders.
- **Solution:** Verdant keeps each plant's watering interval and last-watered date, then sorts the collection by urgency. Owners can mark a plant watered, add one, or remove one. This prototype stores data only in the current browser.
- **Revenue:** Start by testing a free, ad-free product. If users return regularly, test an optional paid tier for useful extras such as multi-device backup, shared household care, or vacation schedules. Ads are easy to imagine but likely earn little with a small audience and can make a calm, trust-based utility feel cluttered.

### Monetization recommendation

Do not build an advertising business first. Validate repeat use and willingness to pay with an ad-free free tier and a small optional subscription. A plant-sitter marketplace could become a later experiment for vacation coverage, but it is a two-sided marketplace: it needs enough trusted sitters and enough nearby plant owners at the same time. It also brings identity checks, reliability, safety, support, and payment costs. Start by interviewing local plant owners and sitters or testing a manually coordinated pilot before building bookings or taking a commission. Do not promise vetted sitters until a real screening process exists.

## 3. Network effects

Does your product get more valuable as more people use it?

- Is it a **one-sided** or **two-sided** market? Who are the sides?
- Where does the extra value come from as the network grows?
- How would you solve the "empty network" problem at launch?
- A future sitter marketplace would be a **two-sided** market: plant owners need nearby available sitters, and sitters need enough local requests. More suitable matches can improve the service, but growth is local, not automatically global. The current prototype is a one-person plant tracker and has no network effect yet; do not claim otherwise.
- To address the marketplace's empty-network problem, pilot in one neighborhood or campus, recruit a small group of sitters first, and manually match a few requests. Point to a future booking flow only after the pilot proves that customers and sitters both return.
